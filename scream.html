SCREAM

From a College Web Project to an Offline Peer-to-Peer Social Platform

1. The Beginning of SCREAM

SCREAM began in 2024 as a college minor project created by Alwin Madhu and Nejin Bejoy.

At that time, our college required us to develop the project as a web application using technologies such as PHP and HTML. Because of those academic requirements, the first version of SCREAM was built as a conventional server-based web application.

The original objective was to create a social-media-style platform where users could create, share, and interact with information.

However, the idea behind SCREAM was always broader than the first implementation.

From the beginning, we were interested in a fundamental question:

What if a social platform could continue to function even when conventional Internet connectivity was unavailable?

At that stage, we did not have enough technical knowledge, experience, or freedom within the college project to fully implement that concept. We were expected to remain within the PHP/web-development requirements, and our understanding of mobile development, peer-to-peer networking, and distributed systems was still developing.

So the first SCREAM remained a web application.

But the idea did not end there.

1.1 Nejin Bejoy's Role in SCREAM

Nejin Bejoy was an important part of the early development of SCREAM during our college period. He worked with me on the original college minor project, where we developed the first version of SCREAM using PHP and HTML.

His involvement was particularly connected to the project's original college-stage development and provided support during the creation of the initial web application.

As SCREAM later moved into a completely different stage of development, the project required a substantial transition from a PHP/HTML web application into a mobile application/APK, followed by experimentation with offline communication and peer-to-peer networking.

During this later phase, Nejin Bejoy was occupied with other responsibilities and was not as actively involved in the mobile development and P2P experimentation. I therefore continued the transition and carried out much of the subsequent development, implementation, and testing myself.

This distinction is important to the project's history: Nejin Bejoy was part of SCREAM's original college development, while the later mobile, APK, and P2P evolution was primarily continued independently by Alwin Madhu.

2. The Original Vision

A conventional social-media platform generally depends on a communication path such as:

User → Internet → Central Server → Internet → User

This architecture is extremely effective when Internet infrastructure is available.

However, it also creates a dependency on that infrastructure.

If Internet connectivity becomes unavailable, unreliable, or inaccessible, users may lose access to the platform even when nearby devices are physically capable of communicating with each other.

That led us to consider another possibility:

User → Mobile Device → Nearby Device → Other Users

Instead of treating the Internet as the only communication layer, we wanted to explore whether mobile devices themselves could participate in the communication network.

This was not a concept that appeared suddenly in 2026.

The underlying idea had already existed within SCREAM from its early development in 2024.

3. The Limitations of the Original College Project

The first SCREAM implementation was shaped by the environment in which it was created.

We had to work with the technologies expected by our college, and the project had to satisfy the requirements of a minor academic project.

At the same time, we were still learning.

We did not yet have the technical experience required to immediately implement a sophisticated mobile peer-to-peer system.

Therefore, the PHP/HTML version was an important first step.

It allowed us to establish the fundamental concept of SCREAM as a social platform while giving us a foundation that could later be re-engineered.

The original web application was not the final destination.

It was the starting point.

4. Reconsidering SCREAM

During 2025 and early 2026, our technical knowledge and interests developed further.

We began looking at the original project differently.

Instead of asking only:

How can we improve the SCREAM website?

we began asking:

Can we transform SCREAM into something that does not fundamentally depend on a conventional Internet connection?

That question led us toward:

  • Mobile application development
  • Peer-to-peer networking
  • Local device communication
  • Offline-first architecture
  • Local data storage
  • Device discovery
  • Data synchronization
  • Distributed communication

This represented a significant architectural change.

We were no longer simply improving a PHP application.

We were rethinking what SCREAM could actually be.

5. May 2026 — The Mobile APK Transition

One of the most important milestones in SCREAM's history happened in May 2026.

During my birthday month, I personally began transforming the original SCREAM project from its PHP/HTML web implementation into a mobile application/APK.

This was not simply a matter of putting the existing website into an APK.

The intention was to create a genuine mobile implementation and begin experimenting with a different communication architecture.

The new direction focused on:

  • Mobile devices as communication endpoints
  • Local communication between devices
  • Peer-to-peer networking
  • Offline operation
  • Local data exchange
  • Social networking without assuming continuous Internet connectivity

The first mobile implementation was built and experimentally tested.

This is an important part of the project's chronology because it establishes that the mobile/P2P direction was already being developed before the 2026 New Delhi student protests became part of our real-world observations.

In other words:

The New Delhi protests did not cause SCREAM to become a mobile application. The mobile transition was already happening.

6. The 2026 New Delhi Student Protests

Later in 2026, we monitored the student protests taking place in New Delhi and the communication conditions surrounding those events.

We were particularly interested in the broader technological question of what happens to communication when conventional connectivity becomes unreliable, restricted, or unavailable during large-scale situations.

This observation was important to us, but its role needs to be described accurately.

  • The protests did not inspire SCREAM.
  • The protests did not create the offline/P2P concept.
  • The protests did not cause the APK conversion.

Those developments had already happened.

Instead, the New Delhi events became another real-world situation through which we could examine a problem that SCREAM had already been exploring.

The question was not political.

It was technical:

What happens to ordinary digital communication when conventional Internet connectivity is no longer available?

That question made the practical value of resilient communication systems more visible.

7. Why Real-World Situations Matter to SCREAM

SCREAM is not designed around a single protest, conflict, country, or political situation.

While developing the project, we began observing different situations around the world where conventional communication infrastructure could become unreliable.

These include situations involving:

  • Internet infrastructure failures
  • Telecommunications disruptions
  • Natural disasters
  • Power failures
  • Large-scale emergencies
  • Physical isolation
  • Conflict-affected areas
  • Unreliable or intermittent connectivity

We also monitored developments associated with different global conflicts and crisis situations, including the Russia–Ukraine war and developments involving Iran and the United States, among other situations.

The purpose of monitoring these events was not to take a political position.

We were interested in the communication and infrastructure problems that can occur in such environments.

Different situations allow us to ask the same technical question from different perspectives:

If conventional communication infrastructure becomes unavailable, what alternatives could exist?

That is directly relevant to the research and development of SCREAM.

8. SCREAM Is About Communication Resilience

As the project developed, the original idea became more clearly defined.

SCREAM is exploring communication resilience.

The goal is to investigate whether a social platform can remain useful when conventional Internet connectivity is unavailable or unreliable.

Instead of assuming:

Internet + Central Server = Social Network

SCREAM explores the possibility of:

Mobile Devices + Local Networking + Peer-to-Peer Communication = Social Network

The Internet does not necessarily have to disappear from the architecture entirely.

Instead, the idea is to provide additional communication paths.

When Internet connectivity is available, conventional networking can be used.

When it is unavailable, local peer-to-peer communication could potentially allow nearby devices to continue exchanging information.

9. The Engineering Challenges

Moving from a conventional web application to a peer-to-peer mobile platform creates a completely different set of technical challenges.

Peer Discovery
How can nearby devices discover one another without relying entirely on a central server?

Communication
How can information be transferred reliably between devices?

Offline Storage
How should posts, profiles, messages, and other information be stored locally?

Synchronization
What happens when two devices reconnect after being offline?

Data Propagation
Can information travel through multiple devices without requiring a permanent central server?

Scalability
How can the network remain useful as the number of participating devices increases?

Reliability
How can the system handle devices joining, leaving, disconnecting, or moving out of range?

User Experience
How can all of this networking complexity remain invisible to ordinary users?

These challenges are now part of the core technical direction of SCREAM.

10. The Goal Is Still a Social Network

Although the underlying architecture is technically complex, SCREAM is not intended to feel like a networking experiment.

The objective is to provide a familiar social-media experience.

A user should ideally be able to:

Open SCREAM → discover nearby users → create a post → view content → interact → communicate → exchange information

without needing to understand how the underlying peer-to-peer network operates.

The complexity should remain underneath the interface.

This is why we continue to place significant emphasis on:

  • User interface
  • User experience
  • Feature design
  • Performance
  • Scalability
  • Reliability
  • Ease of use

The ambition is not simply to prove that two phones can communicate.

The ambition is to explore whether peer-to-peer technology can support an actual social networking experience.

The relationship can be summarized very simply.

It is not:

SCREAM → original concept → PHP/HTML project → mobile/P2P idea → APK development and testing → New Delhi protests observed → broader understanding of communication resilience → continued SCREAM development

The protests were context, not origin.

13. The Broader Research Context

Our interest in SCREAM comes from a broader observation about modern communication.

People have become heavily dependent on Internet infrastructure for almost every form of digital communication.

That dependency is normally invisible because the infrastructure works most of the time.

But when something interrupts it, the dependency becomes obvious.

A network may fail because of:

  • Infrastructure damage
  • Natural disasters
  • Power failures
  • Network congestion
  • Telecommunications disruption
  • Physical isolation
  • Emergency conditions
  • Other unforeseen circumstances

SCREAM explores whether a portion of social communication could continue through local device-to-device networking in such circumstances.

The project is therefore not about replacing the Internet.

It is about exploring what could exist alongside it.

14. The Long-Term Vision

The long-term vision for SCREAM is an open-source, mobile social platform capable of adapting to different connectivity conditions.

When Internet connectivity is available
SCREAM can use conventional Internet infrastructure where appropriate.

When Internet connectivity is unavailable
Nearby devices could potentially communicate through peer-to-peer technologies.

When connectivity is intermittent
Local storage and synchronization mechanisms could allow information to remain available until connectivity is restored.

When users are physically close
Their devices could potentially communicate directly without requiring every interaction to travel through a remote server.

This creates a model where the application is not completely dependent on a single communication infrastructure.

15. Why SCREAM Is Becoming More Interesting

What began as a college minor project has gradually become an exploration of several areas of computer science and software engineering:

  • Mobile computing
  • Peer-to-peer networking
  • Distributed systems
  • Offline-first architecture
  • Local communication
  • Data synchronization
  • Decentralized systems
  • Resilient communication
  • Social networking
  • Human-centered software design

The interesting part of SCREAM is not simply that it is a social application.

The interesting part is the question underneath it:

Can social communication become more resilient by allowing mobile devices to communicate directly with one another?

That is the problem we are continuing to explore.

17. Final Project Story

SCREAM began in 2024 as a college minor project created by Alwin Madhu and Nejin Bejoy. The first version was developed using PHP and HTML because of the technical and academic requirements of the college project. At that time, SCREAM was a conventional web-based social platform, but the idea behind it was already larger than the implementation.

From the beginning, we were interested in the possibility of creating a social platform that would not be completely dependent on conventional Internet infrastructure. We imagined a system where nearby mobile devices could communicate directly with each other and where social communication could potentially continue even when conventional Internet connectivity was unavailable.

At the time, limitations in our technical knowledge and the requirements of the college project meant that we could not fully implement that idea. The PHP/HTML application therefore became the first stage of SCREAM.

As our technical knowledge developed during 2025 and early 2026, we began reconsidering the architecture. In May 2026, during my birthday month, I personally began transforming the original SCREAM project into a mobile APK/application. This was more than simply converting a website into a mobile package. The objective was to explore a new architecture based around mobile devices, local communication, offline operation, and peer-to-peer networking. The first mobile implementation was built and experimentally tested before the 2026 New Delhi student protests became part of our real-world observations.

Later, we monitored the student protests in New Delhi and the communication conditions surrounding them. These events did not inspire SCREAM, did not create its offline/P2P concept, and did not cause us to convert the project into a mobile application. The mobile transition was already underway. Instead, the New Delhi events became another real-world situation through which we could examine the communication-resilience problem that SCREAM had already been exploring.

We also continued observing situations around the world where conventional communication infrastructure can become unreliable or unavailable, including conflict-affected regions, disasters, infrastructure failures, and other large-scale emergencies. These observations were not about taking a political position. They helped us understand the practical environments in which resilient communication technologies could potentially become useful.

Through this process, SCREAM evolved from a college PHP/HTML web project into an experimental mobile, offline-first, peer-to-peer social networking platform. The project's central question became much broader than the original academic assignment:

How can people continue to communicate, share information, and maintain a social network when conventional Internet connectivity is unavailable or unreliable?

SCREAM is not designed for one particular protest, country, conflict, or political situation. It is an exploration of a technological possibility: creating a social networking experience that can use conventional Internet infrastructure when it is available while also exploring local peer-to-peer communication when it is not.

The long-term vision is to make communication more resilient without making the technology more complicated for the user. The goal is to combine the familiarity of a modern social network with the possibilities of mobile peer-to-peer and offline-first communication.

What began in 2024 as a college minor project has therefore become an ongoing exploration of mobile computing, distributed systems, peer-to-peer networking, offline-first architecture, data synchronization, and resilient communication.

SCREAM started as a social-media project. It evolved into an experiment in what social communication could look like when the Internet is no longer the only path between people.
